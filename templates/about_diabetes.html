Understanding Diabetes Mellitus

A simple, plain-language guide to blood glucose, how insulin works in the body, the key differences between types, and practical daily prevention steps.

Medical Disclaimer: This is not a medical diagnosis. Please consult a doctor for official clinical diagnosis and treatment.

What is Diabetes?

Glucose & Insulin balance

When we eat, our digestive system breaks food down into glucose (blood sugar), which enters the bloodstream. The pancreas produces a vital hormone called insulin, which acts like a key, allowing cells to absorb glucose and use it for energy.

In diabetes, the body either doesn't make enough insulin or can't use the insulin it makes effectively. This causes glucose to build up in the bloodstream over time, potentially leading to long-term health complications if left unmanaged.

Type 1 vs. Type 2 Diabetes

Key clinical differences
Type 1 Diabetes:
  • Autoimmune condition where the body stops making insulin.
  • Usually diagnosed in children and young adults.
  • Requires daily insulin therapy.
Type 2 Diabetes:
  • Cells become resistant to insulin (insulin resistance).
  • Accounts for 90-95% of all cases worldwide.
  • Closely linked to lifestyle, diet, weight, and genetics.

Common Warning Symptoms

Early signs to watch out for
  • Frequent Urination (Polyuria): Needing to urinate often, especially waking up multiple times during the night.
  • Excessive Thirst (Polydipsia): Feeling persistently thirsty even after drinking plenty of fluids.
  • Unexplained Fatigue: Feeling worn out and lethargic because cells aren't getting enough usable energy.
  • Blurry Vision: High sugar levels draw fluid from eye lenses, making focusing difficult.
  • Slow-Healing Cuts: Minor scrapes, wounds, or sores that take longer than normal to heal.

Practical Prevention Tips

Simple habits that lower Type 2 risk
  • Stay Active Daily: Aim for at least 30 minutes of moderate exercise (brisk walking, cycling, or yoga) 5 days a week.
  • Choose Whole Foods: Replace refined white flour, white rice, and sugary sodas with whole grains, lentils, green vegetables, and nuts.
  • Maintain a Healthy Weight: Losing just 5-7% of excess body weight can reduce Type 2 diabetes risk by more than 50%.
  • Stay Well Hydrated: Drink clean water instead of fruit juices with added sugar, sweet teas, or energy drinks.
  • Get Screened Periodically: Regular fasting blood glucose checks help catch pre-diabetes early when it is still reversible.
